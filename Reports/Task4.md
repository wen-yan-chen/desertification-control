# Holding the Taklamakan Green Belt: A Practical Operating Plan

**To:** County officials responsible for forestry, water resources, and project finance

Dear Directors and Project Managers,

Our study of the Yutian–Minfeng section links a 15-year construction and operating plan with a further 50-year disaster stress test. The baseline schedule below offers a practical starting point, conditional on reliable water deliveries, funded maintenance, and viable electricity sales.

**Secure water before expanding.** Confirm monthly deliveries before approving new planting. Baseline May demand reaches 95% of monthly delivery capacity, making spring supply the immediate bottleneck. Service pumps and drip lines before May; postpone expansion if existing stands lack water and publish drought allocation rules.

**Make maintenance accountable.** Assign each block a supervisor and check survival after planting and the first summer. Increase irrigation as stands mature, check soil moisture before changing deliveries, and correct irrigation faults or burial damage before replanting.

**Define who pays.** The baseline requires about CNY 44.3 million in planting and PV capital during years 1–4. During years 5–15, modelled annual PV income of CNY 4.1–4.3 million exceeds annual upkeep and water costs of CNY 1.8–2.0 million. We recommend public funding for planting and essential water infrastructure, with the operator responsible for upkeep. Earmark viable PV revenue for maintenance, equipment replacement, and a disaster reserve. Itemise annual costs, review spending quarterly, and secure funding gaps before expansion; these revenues depend on electricity prices and operating conditions.

**Expected benefits.** Sustained care can maintain shelter near roads, settlements, and farmland while supporting recurring local maintenance work. In the baseline 50-year stress scenario, continued irrigation preserves 65.5% of the cumulative vegetation-cover benefit of the disaster-free case, showing that damaged stands can still provide substantial long-term value.

**Protect communities and report results.** Prioritise vulnerable sections near roads, settlements, and farmland, and include local workers in maintenance contracts. Coordinate irrigation with household and agricultural needs. Publish vegetation condition, water deliveries, spending, and unfinished repairs annually, with a contact for public reports.

| When | Practical action | Lead responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Years 1–2 | Plant 3 square kilometres of shelterbelt, subject to confirmed water and maintenance funding. | Water authority and planting contractor |
| Years 1–4 | Install 9.5 MW of PV only with grid access, viable returns, and sufficient budget. | Project operator and finance department |
| Years 5–15 | Fund upkeep from operating income; inspect irrigation before May and audit spending quarterly. | Operator and irrigation team |
| Year 16 onward | Continue monitoring and funded upkeep; inspect after droughts or storms and repair recoverable stands. | Monitoring and maintenance teams |

Please designate a lead coordinator to agree on the delivery schedule, maintenance responsibilities, and funding plan before the next planting season. Satellite warnings should trigger field inspections and repairs so that recoverable stands remain in service.

Yours sincerely,  
The Modelling Team
